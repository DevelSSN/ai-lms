# Photosynthesis

## Overview

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and certain
bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy in the form of glucose. The
overall reaction is carbon dioxide plus water, in the presence of light and
chlorophyll, producing glucose and oxygen.

## Light-Dependent Reactions

These reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast. Light
energy splits water molecules (photolysis), releasing oxygen gas and producing
ATP and NADPH. The energy carriers ATP and NADPH are then used in the next
stage.

## Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle)

The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma of the chloroplast. Using the ATP and
NADPH from the light-dependent stage, carbon dioxide is fixed into glucose
through a series of enzyme-catalyzed steps. The enzyme RuBisCO catalyzes the
first step, carbon fixation.

## Key Concepts

- Chlorophyll is the pigment that captures light energy.
- Photolysis is the splitting of water molecules by light.
- RuBisCO catalyzes carbon fixation in the Calvin cycle.
- The products of photosynthesis are glucose and oxygen.
